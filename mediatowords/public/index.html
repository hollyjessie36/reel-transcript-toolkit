How it works

With Reel link selected by default, paste the URL of a public Instagram Reel or single video post. Choose the spoken language if you know it, or leave Auto-detect selected. Press Transcribe Reel; the server retrieves the public video, then your browser recognizes its speech. Review the text and copy it or export TXT or SRT. For a recording already on your device, switch to Upload file, select a file no larger than 250 MiB, and press Transcribe file.

Processing uses the device you are working on, so available memory and the length of the recording affect whether it finishes. If a recording cannot be decoded, export a compatible audio copy from your media editor and try that file.

When the transcript appears, play the recording and correct the editable lines. Check names, numbers and the final spoken sentence before relying on the text. Copy the words for a document, download TXT for timestamped notes, or download SRT to import subtitles into an editor. Save your result before leaving or reloading the page.

What you can transcribe: audio to text and video to text

For audio recordings, use your own interviews, meetings or voice notes. For video recordings, use exports you have permission to process. The file picker accepts audio and video; successful decoding depends on the browser and the audio codec inside the file. The tool does not promise a fixed list of extensions, batch processing or a guaranteed recording duration.

The language selector provides Auto-detect and twelve named choices, listed in the FAQ below. This is speech transcription, not a translation workflow. Background music, quiet speech and overlapping speakers can make the result less reliable. Review against the original audio, especially if you intend to quote somebody or publish subtitles.

Reel links that fail, and what to do instead

A public link is a starting point, not a guarantee that a recording can be retrieved. Before trying again, read the message under the tool and decide whether the failure concerns access, downloading, file size or speech recognition. That distinction helps you choose a useful next step instead of submitting the same unavailable address repeatedly.

Private accounts or a login requirement

A recording that is visible only after signing in cannot be fetched through this public-link workflow, and the tool does not ask for your Instagram password or borrow your account session. If you control the post, consider whether making it public is appropriate; otherwise, ask its author for an original file you are allowed to use. Keep private material private when that is the creator’s intention, and use a permitted local copy rather than trying to bypass the restriction.

Deleted posts or regional restrictions

Reopen the exact post address to check that the recording still exists and is available where you are; an old message or bookmark can point to a post that has since disappeared. Regional restrictions may also prevent the retrieval service from reaching a video that opens in your own browser, so local visibility alone does not prove that the download will work. Confirm the source with its creator and request an accessible, authorized copy if the public post cannot be retrieved.

Size limits and interrupted downloads

The tool checks a 250 MiB size limit for downloaded media and selected local files, and it displays a message when that check fails; it does not impose a fixed number of video minutes in the browser code. A download can also stop because the remote service takes too long, which is a retrieval timeout rather than a statement about the recording’s duration. For an oversized video, export a smaller audio track or split the recording into suitable parts, then select Upload file; choosing a local file does not remove the same size check.

The link opens, but transcription fails

If you can obtain a permitted local video copy, switch to Upload file and select it to avoid repeating the public-link retrieval step. If the file still fails, inspect the new message: a decoding problem calls for a compatible audio export, while a memory problem may call for closing unused tabs or processing a shorter recording. Listen to the saved source before retrying, because muted or unintelligible audio cannot produce a dependable transcript simply by changing input modes.

Once a retry succeeds, compare the recognized text with the exact source you used. Keep any shortened recording and its exported subtitles together: captions made from a trimmed file may not align with an earlier, longer version. Save the reviewed result before you close the page.

What happens to your file

With Reel link selected, you send the public post address to the retrieval service. The server attempts to obtain that public video and returns the media for processing in your browser. This route therefore includes a server download step; it is different from selecting a recording already stored on your device, and it cannot retrieve content that requires your personal login.

With Upload file selected, the page reads the recording you choose and performs speech recognition locally in the browser. The first use loads the recognition software and a speech model over the network, so local processing does not mean the page works without internet access. Keep the tab open while the model prepares and the recording is processed.

Both routes produce editable transcript lines and the same text-download choices. Review the wording before saving, choose TXT for notes or SRT for timed subtitles, and retain your downloaded copy. This description explains the two processing paths; it does not make a blanket promise that every action on the website happens without a server.

Audio and video to text FAQ

Will my file be uploaded to a server?

In Upload file mode, the recording stays on your device and speech recognition runs in your browser. The page still downloads software and a speech model, so first use needs an internet connection. Reel link mode is different: our server retrieves the public Instagram video before your browser transcribes it.

Which file formats are supported?

Choose an audio or video file that your browser can decode. There is no fixed extension whitelist in this tool: a filename ending in MP4 or another familiar extension does not guarantee that its audio codec will work. The maximum file size is 250 MiB, and you process one file at a time.

Which languages can I choose?

Choose Auto-detect or select English, Spanish, French, German, Indonesian, Portuguese, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Arabic, Hindi or Italian. Pick the spoken language when you know it. Recognition quality varies with the recording; a language option does not promise perfect spelling, punctuation or names.

Should I download TXT or SRT?

Choose TXT for a readable transcript with a start-time label on each line. Choose SRT for numbered subtitle cues with start and end times. Copy text gives you the words without timestamp labels. Review the editable lines before copying or downloading, because your corrections are included in the result.

Is audio to text free?

Yes. You can transcribe a recording without an account or payment, then copy the result or download TXT and SRT. Your selected file is recognized on your device, but the page needs an internet connection to download the speech model, especially on first use.

Can I turn a video into text without uploading it?

Yes, if you choose Upload file. Your browser reads the saved video and recognizes its speech locally. The Reel link option follows a different route: our server first retrieves a public Instagram video, then your browser transcribes the returned media.

How accurate is the audio to text result?

It depends on the spoken language, noise and whether people talk over each other. We do not promise an accuracy percentage. Listen to the recording while reviewing the editable transcript, and check names, numbers and the final sentence before you use it.

Is it legal to transcribe a recording I did not record?

Permission and the intended use matter. A video being public does not automatically give you the right to republish its words. Check the creator’s permission and applicable exceptions before sharing a transcript. The U.S. Copyright Office explains that fair use depends on the circumstances; see its guidance on using another person’s work.
