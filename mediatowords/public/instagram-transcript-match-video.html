How do I match an Instagram transcript SRT to the right video?

Keep the SRT beside the exact video export, check that its final cue ends within the clip, then compare the first and last spoken lines in a preview. Use a shared version label such as clip-v2.mp4 and clip-v2.srt. A matching name helps you organize files; it does not verify the words or timing.

Our two-clip pairing experiment

On November 11, 2026, we generated two silent, solid-color MP4 clips with FFmpeg: A lasts 8 seconds and B lasts 12 seconds. We read the finished files with FFmpeg to confirm both durations. These are controlled fixtures, not downloaded Instagram videos, and this experiment does not measure transcription accuracy.

We authored two SRT files. Both contain a first cue from 1 to 3 seconds and a final cue starting at 5 seconds. A's final cue ends at 7 seconds; B's ends at 11 seconds. Both use the same two sample sentences so that different wording cannot explain the timing result.

What should I record for my real Instagram video?

Save the export version, language and subtitle filename together. If you trim or replace the video, review its paired subtitles again. Preview the first and last spoken passages and any edited section against the audio. Two exports can share a duration while containing different speech.

Does renaming the SRT fix a mismatch?

No. Renaming changes the label, not the subtitle text or timestamps. Return to the correct source export and check the captions there.

Can a video fingerprint prove the subtitles are correct?

A stored fingerprint can help identify the same video bytes later. It does not prove that the subtitles accurately describe those bytes. You still need a speech and timing review.
