How do I correct words in an Instagram transcript?

Listen to the disputed phrase, edit the matching transcript line, then download a fresh TXT or SRT and check it. Text corrections are included in the export, while the existing subtitle times stay unchanged.

How to diagnose the problem

A spelling error and a timing error require different checks. If the subtitle appears at the right moment but a name is wrong, edit the wording. If the words are correct but appear too early or late, keep the text and inspect the timing in your subtitle editor. Changing a word will not move its cue to a different point in the recording.

Keep the original audio available while you review. Recognition can produce a plausible sentence that differs from what the speaker actually said. Pay particular attention to names, quantities, dates and negations, because a small textual change can alter the meaning. Do not replace uncertain speech with a more polished claim merely because it sounds better in writing.

Three steps to fix it

Step 1: listen and verify the intended wording

Play a few seconds before and after the disputed phrase. Context may distinguish a product name from an ordinary word or clarify whether a speaker said a number in full. Repeat the passage if necessary, and check a reliable spelling source when the subject is a named person, organization or technical term. The audio remains the reference for what was said.

Locate the matching editable line using its start-time label. The same word can appear more than once, so identify the occurrence by its surrounding sentence rather than by spelling alone. If only one occurrence is wrong, correct that occurrence. A broad search-and-replace in a downloaded file can damage another sentence where the original word was appropriate.

Step 2: edit the line without losing its context

Change the text in the matching transcript field. Keep punctuation useful for reading, but avoid inserting new factual information that the recording did not contain. If you need an explanatory note for readers, keep it separate from a verbatim quotation. Read the entire corrected sentence before moving on to the next line.

This page includes your edited text when you copy or download. It does not automatically retime a subtitle because you added or removed words. A longer correction may need more reading time than the old cue provides. Check that issue in your video editor rather than assuming an accurate sentence is already a finished subtitle. Save a download before closing or reloading the page.

Step 3: export again and inspect the saved file

Choose Download TXT for notes or Download SRT for timed captions. Make a new download after the correction; an older file in your Downloads folder does not update itself when you edit the page. Use a version label that makes the corrected export easy to distinguish from the first draft. Open that exact file and search for the corrected phrase.

For SRT, import the saved file into your video editor and preview the corresponding speech. Check both the spelling and whether the full line is readable within its cue. If the text wraps badly or covers an important part of the frame, adjust subtitle presentation in the editor. Keep a copy of the corrected SRT with the matching video export.

What if the same mistake appears throughout the recording?

Review each occurrence in context before applying the same correction everywhere. A proper name may need consistent spelling, while an ordinary word can be correct in one sentence and wrong in another. For a long recording, keep a short review list of confirmed names and spellings. Verify the beginning, middle and ending after edits so that a large revision does not conceal a missed passage.

If you cannot hear the phrase clearly, leave yourself an uncertainty note and obtain clarification from the creator when possible. The goal is a faithful transcript, not a confident guess. Once the wording is resolved, preserve the reviewed version and avoid overwriting it with an unchecked automatic export.
